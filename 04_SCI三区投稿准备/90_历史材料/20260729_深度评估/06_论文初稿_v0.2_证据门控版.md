## Abstract

Individually ordered eigenfunctions are an ill-posed learning target at an exact eigenvalue degeneracy because every unitary rotation yields an equally valid eigenbasis for the same invariant spectral subspace. We study a label-free neural variational solver for an isolated rank-two low-energy cluster of a parameterized two-dimensional Bloch–Schrödinger operator. Rather than assigning persistent identities to two crossing bands, the implemented prototype predicts an orthonormal frame whose primary interpretation is the associated basis-invariant spectral projector. Its verified dual-path orthogonalization evaluates the global quadrature transform on a coordinate-detached branch, preserving derivatives with respect to network parameters without contaminating pointwise spatial automatic differentiation. ## 1. Introduction

Neural methods for partial differential equations are most often presented as approximations of an initial- or boundary-value solution field. Differential eigenproblems pose a different set of difficulties. The zero field can trivially satisfy a homogeneous residual, multiple outputs may collapse to the same eigenfunction, and a small residual does not determine which part of the spectrum has been recovered. Neural eigensolvers consequently employ normalization, Rayleigh quotients, deflation, orthogonality, trace principles, trial subspaces, or orbital-minimization objectives.

Parameter dependence adds an amortization problem. A single network is asked to represent spectral information across coefficients, geometries, or Bloch wavevectors. Such a map can be useful when a design or uncertainty-quantification loop requires many related solves. Supervised reduced-order models and subspace regression learn this map from high-fidelity snapshots, but generating and consistently labeling these snapshots already requires an eigensolver and a mode-tracking policy.

Eigenvalue crossings expose a more fundamental representation issue. Suppose the first two eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator coincide at a parameter value. Their two-dimensional invariant subspace may be uniquely defined and remain separated from the remainder of the spectrum, yet no individual orthonormal basis of that subspace is preferred. Each basis is equivalent under a unitary rotation. Sorting two network outputs by predicted eigenvalue cannot remove this ambiguity at the crossing and may create artificial discontinuities in the learning target.

This issue is intrinsic to periodic operators. Honeycomb Schrödinger systems possess symmetry-protected band degeneracies and conical intersections under suitable spectral conditions. Equation-driven networks have already been used for periodic quantum systems, honeycomb band calculations, and Bloch–Floquet dispersion. Likewise, trace-based neural eigensolvers, neural trial-subspace methods, conditional eigenvalue networks, and parameterized mode-switching methods are established. The presence of a Bloch equation, a Ky Fan objective, multiple neural functions, or a Dirac point is therefore not by itself a contribution.

We investigate the narrower question of whether one label-free coordinate network can learn an isolated, internally degenerate rank-two Bloch cluster as a basis-invariant object across both wavevector and potential parameters. The network outputs a frame, but its physical prediction is the projector onto the frame. A low-order Bloch model supplies a problem-specific subspace, and the neural component learns only the correction that the low-fidelity model misses. This decomposition permits direct controls: anchor only, anchor plus Rayleigh–Ritz, incorrect anchors, an unanchored block network, and supervised eigenspace regression.

A second difficulty is the interaction between global orthogonalization and pointwise automatic differentiation. If quadrature-based Gram coefficients are fully detached, the backward pass is not the derivative of the composed variational objective with respect to network parameters. If they are differentiated through the coordinate grid, spatial derivatives acquire artificial cross-sample terms. We address this with a dual-path construction: a coordinate-active branch supplies the PDE field and a coordinate-detached, shared-weight branch supplies the global Gram/retraction transform. The transform remains differentiable with respect to the network parameters while acting as a spatial constant.

The intended contributions are:

1. a projector-first formulation of an internally crossing, isolated Bloch spectral cluster, with training and evaluation invariant under rotations inside the target cluster;
2. a symmetry-adapted low-fidelity subspace plus Grassmann neural-correction model trained directly from the differential operator without eigenspace labels;
3. a dual-path quadrature retraction that separates parameter differentiation from pointwise spatial differentiation;
4. a gap-aware analysis connecting Ky Fan excess, projector error, approximation error, and quadrature error;
5. a two-potential benchmark with measured-gap near-crossing points, strict OOD points, strong neural subspace baselines, low-order plane-wave controls, and consumer-GPU reproducibility.

These are hypotheses until the formal experiment is complete. The current local evidence supports the dual-path correction as an engineering mechanism but does not establish superiority over recent subspace methods or a well-optimized plane-wave solver.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Neural eigensolvers

Ben-Shaul et al. proposed an unsupervised end-to-end treatment of multiple eigenpairs of self-adjoint differential operators. Yang et al. embedded power and inverse-power iterations in neural approximations, while Rowan et al. combined Rayleigh quotients with Gram–Schmidt for engineering eigenproblems. Guo et al. further introduced deep Rayleigh quotient iteration. These studies preclude a claim of being the first unsupervised or multi-eigenpair neural differential eigensolver.

Trace and subspace methods are especially close. Wang and Xie use tensor neural networks and generalized trace minimization to approximate several leading eigenpairs. Dai, Fan, and Sheng learn an orthogonal neural trial subspace and then solve a projected Galerkin problem. Ryu et al. revisit orbital minimization as an unordered neural block objective. The present work must therefore be evaluated against these formulations under matched budgets.

### 2.2 Parameter-dependent eigenspaces and multiplicity

Conditional neural eigenvalue solvers, Gaussian-process reduced-order models, supervised subspace regression, and Deep Eigenspace Networks learn parameter-dependent spectral information. Grubišić et al. and Dölz and Ebert show why isolated eigenspaces, rather than particular eigenvectors, are the stable object under internal multiplicity. Shape Space Spectra is the most dangerous parallel work: it trains physics-only coordinate fields across a continuous shape family and explicitly handles mode multiplicity and crossover through dynamic sorting and causal gradient filtering. Our remaining distinction is not the general handling of crossings; it is the fixed-rank, basis-invariant Bloch projector objective together with a label-free physical correction chart.

### 2.3 Bloch and periodic neural solvers

Hsu et al. solve two-dimensional periodic quantum systems without solution labels. Mian studies honeycomb and Dirac-point Bloch eigensolvers with nearly-free-electron transfer, and Cheng et al. present a journal-level PINN treatment of real and complex Bloch–Floquet dispersion. These works rule out novelty claims based solely on periodic quantum equations or honeycomb structure. Our comparison focuses on whether treating the two crossing bands as an inseparable projector avoids the representation and optimization problems of individual modes.

## 3. Problem Formulation

Let \(s=(s_1,s_2)\in\Omega=[0,2\pi)^2\), \(k=(k_1,k_2)\), and \(\xi\) denote potential parameters. We consider

\[
H_{k,\xi}u
=\left[\frac12(-i\nabla_s+k)^\top
\begin{bmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{bmatrix}
(-i\nabla_s+k)+V(s;\xi)\right]u
=\lambda u,
\]

with periodic values and first derivatives. Let \(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3\le\cdots\). The target is

\[
P_\star(\mu)=U_\star(\mu)U_\star(\mu)^*,
\qquad U_\star^*U_\star=I_2,
\]

where \(\mu=(k,\xi)\). We permit \(\lambda_1=\lambda_2\), but assume an isolated cluster \(g_{\mathrm{ext}}=\lambda_3-\lambda_2\ge\gamma_0>0\) on the declared local parameter region.

Two potential families are used. The harmonic family combines three reciprocal-lattice harmonics and a symmetry-breaking term. The Gaussian family contains periodic localized wells on two sublattices, with amplitude, width, and imbalance parameters. The second family is required to test whether a low-frequency harmonic anchor merely solves a tailored example.

## 4. Method

### 4.1 Periodic coordinate network

The coordinate network receives \((\sin s_1,\cos s_1,\sin s_2,\cos s_2,\mu)\) and returns two complex correction fields. Smooth periodic features enforce value and derivative periodicity. The formal architecture uses matched parameter counts across methods.

### 4.2 Low-fidelity spectral chart

**[ROM/Grassmann implementation pending; this subsection specifies the candidate SCI-Q3 mechanism, not completed code.]**

A small 3- or 9-wave Bloch Hamiltonian produces a parameter-dependent low-fidelity subspace \(A(\mu)\). This subspace is derived from the governing operator and does not use the high-fidelity test eigenspace. The neural field is projected onto its orthogonal complement,

\[
Z_\theta=(I-A A^*)N_\theta,
\]

and retracted through

\[
Q_\theta=(A+Z_\theta)(I+Z_\theta^*Z_\theta)^{-1/2}.
\]

The chart is valid while the target and anchor remain transverse. A gap- and angle-aware multi-chart rule is used outside this regime. **[Implementation pending.]**

### 4.3 Dual-path differentiation

The raw field is evaluated at the active spatial coordinates. A second shared-weight evaluation uses `stopgrad(s)` to compute global Gram, projection, and inverse-square-root transforms. Consequently, the retraction is differentiated exactly with respect to \(\theta\), while pointwise derivatives with respect to \(s\) do not contain cross-grid terms. The already implemented dual-path MGS is the first verified instance of this construction.

### 4.4 Label-free objective and Ritz recovery

Training minimizes

\[
\mathcal L_{KF}(\theta)
=\mathbb E_\mu\left[\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\theta^*H_\mu Q_\theta)\right].
\]

The current implementation uses the true rank sum. The archived 2026-07-29 mechanism smoke used the rank-normalized objective \(\operatorname{Tr}(Q^*HQ)/r\); for fixed \(r=2\), this positive constant scaling leaves the minimizer and the paired A/B ordering unchanged.

At inference, the \(2\times2\) Ritz matrix \(Q_\theta^*H_\mu Q_\theta\) supplies an unordered eigenvalue pair. The primary field prediction is \(P_\theta=Q_\theta Q_\theta^*\).

## 5. Analysis

The projector, Ky Fan objective, Ritz spectrum, and principal-angle metrics are invariant under \(Q\mapsto QR\), \(R\in U(2)\). We use

\[
e_P=\frac{\|P_\theta-P_\star\|_F}{\sqrt{2r}},\qquad
\Delta_{KF}=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\theta^*H_\mu Q_\theta)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_j,
\qquad \overline\Delta_{KF}=\Delta_{KF}/r.
\]

Under an external gap, a fixed-parameter estimate has the form

\[
e_P(\mu)^2\le\frac{\Delta_{KF}(\mu)}{r\,g_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mu)}
=\frac{\overline\Delta_{KF}(\mu)}{g_{\mathrm{ext}}(\mu)}.
\]

The final paper will state the precise operator-domain assumptions and separate continuous and discrete quadrature statements. A parameter-averaged bound will add approximation and quadrature terms; it will not infer network generalization from the pointwise inequality alone.

## 6. Experiments

The final test contains 640 points over two potential families: IID hidden, exact cluster, measured-gap near cluster, strict OOD, and gap scan. Final test data are generated once, hashed, and never used for smoke or hyperparameter selection. Common parameter and coordinate streams are separated from model-initialization randomness.

The primary endpoint is near-cluster projector sine error. Comparators include high-accuracy and low-order PWE, anchor-only and anchor-plus-Ritz controls, ordered and Rayleigh–GS neural eigensolvers, generalized trace, neural Galerkin, orbital minimization, causal sorting, and supervised Grassmann regression. The main comparison uses ten shared seeds; long-tail ablations use at least three.

## 7. Development Evidence and Formal Results Gate

The 2026-07-29 local smoke test used 12 harmonic points and three new MPS seeds. Dual-path MGS reduced mean projector error from 0.221406 to 0.127776 and improved all 36 seed–point pairs, with a 1.35× training-time multiplier and maximum orthogonality error below \(2\times10^{-7}\). It also outperformed a 9-mode PWE approximation on this small suite, whereas a 25-mode PWE attained 0.024473 and remained substantially more accurate.

These development data justify a CUDA pilot but are not submission results. The final manuscript must replace this section with frozen two-family results or explicitly present it as a pilot study. No superiority, speed, generality, or state-of-the-art claim follows from the current smoke test.

## 8. Discussion

The principal scientific risk is that the problem-specific anchor solves most of a low-frequency harmonic example while a small plane-wave method is both faster and more accurate. The experimental design therefore treats low-order PWE and anchor-only controls as primary comparators, not supplementary baselines. A useful outcome may be an amortized, confidence-aware cluster map for a large parameter ensemble rather than a replacement for direct eigensolvers at a single parameter value.

The method is local in parameter space. A globally smooth frame may be obstructed on a full Brillouin torus even when a projector is well defined. A single Grassmann chart also fails if a target subspace becomes orthogonal to its anchor. These limitations motivate multi-chart anchors and explicit fallback, and they must remain in the final scope statement.
